#+TITLE: train-time.el
#+SUBTITLE: All aboard, without leaving Emacs.

Real-time train arrivals and departures in GNU Emacs, from official
transit data.

* Status

Supported today: every New York City Subway line and the Staten Island
Railway — all 496 stations in MTA's station list — using MTA's public
GTFS-Realtime feeds.  No API key or account is needed.

=M-x train-time-map= is a keyboard-driven text diagram of each line in
the style of =undo-tree-visualize=.  =M-x train-time-home-to-work= and
=M-x train-time-work-to-home= list the next trains between two saved
stations.  Boards and trips show the service alerts, planned work and
elevator/escalator outages that concern them, and =M-x
train-time-alerts= lists a line's alerts in full.  Seoul, Tokyo, favorites, graphical maps and general route
planning are planned but not implemented yet; see [[*Roadmap][Roadmap]].

* Requirements

- GNU Emacs 27.1 or later, built with GnuTLS (standard on most builds).
- Nothing else.  The GTFS-Realtime Protocol Buffers decoder is written
  in Emacs Lisp; no Python, =protoc= or external process is needed.

* Installation

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(add-to-list 'load-path "/path/to/train-time")
(require 'train-time)
#+end_src

* Usage

=M-x train-time= asks for a station (default: Flushing-Main St) and
opens a buffer of upcoming trains.  Candidates show the routes that
stop there, e.g. "Coney Island-Stillwell Av (D F N Q)"; stations that
share a name are told apart by their daytime routes, e.g. "Times
Sq-42 St (7)" and "Times Sq-42 St (N Q R W)".

#+begin_example
 Line  Service  Toward             Destination          Event    Time        In Status     Train
  D             Manhattan          Norwood-205 St       Arrives  15:34       now Tracked    1D 1535+ STL/205
  Q             Manhattan          96 St                Arrives  15:35       now Scheduled  1Q 1535+ STL/962
  F    Local    Manhattan          Jamaica-179 St       Arrives  15:36       now Scheduled  1F 1536 STL/179
  N             Manhattan          Astoria-Ditmars Blvd Arrives  15:41     6 min Scheduled  1N 1541+ STL/DIT

Feed time 15:35:13 EST (4s old)
69 terminating trains hidden  (t to show)
Data: Metropolitan Transportation Authority (MTA)
#+end_example

Route bullets use MTA's colors.  MTA publishes its real time data in
eight feeds grouped by route (1-7 and S, ACE, BDFM, G, JZ, NQRW, L,
SIR); a board fetches every feed serving the station in parallel and
merges them.  The *Service* column says Local or Express only for
routes that have both (the 6, 7 and F and their =<6>=, =<7>=, =<F>=
variants); it is blank for the others.

| Key   | Command                              |
|-------+--------------------------------------|
| =g=   | Refresh now                          |
| =a=   | Toggle auto refresh                  |
| =d=   | Filter by direction                  |
| =s=   | Filter by service (local / express)  |
| =l=   | Filter by line                       |
| =c=   | Clear filters                        |
| =t=   | Show/hide trains terminating here    |
| =S=   | Switch to another station            |
| =RET= | Show trip details in the echo area   |
| =m=   | Show the station on OpenStreetMap    |
| =D=   | Open the board of the destination    |
| =A=   | List the service alerts of the lines |
| =q=   | Quit window                          |

*Evil users:* when Evil is loaded the buffer starts in normal state,
=j=/=k=/=gg=/=G= move as usual, =g r= refreshes, and the other keys
above work unchanged in normal state.  Set =train-time-evil-bindings=
to nil to opt out.  In normal state =g d= opens the destination's
board.

Destinations are links: click one, or press =RET= on it, to open the
board of that station.  Trains that end at the station shown are not
linked.

** Station complexes

At a station with free transfers to others, the board covers the whole
station complex: Grand Central-42 St lists the 7, the 4 5 6 and the S
together, and Times Sq-42 St has nine groups from five platforms,
including the A C E at 42 St-Port Authority Bus Terminal.  Every
station of a complex opens the same board, named after the complex;
the station you came from is listed first.  In =M-x train-time-map=, =RET= on Grand
Central opens it from the 7 as well as from the 4, 5 or 6.

Trains are grouped by platform and direction, with the next
=train-time-group-max-rows= (3) in each:

#+begin_example
 ▾  7   Queens  3 of 13
  <7>  Express  Queens             Flushing-Main St     Arrives  16:13       now Tracked    07 1608+ 34H/MST
  7    Local    Queens             Flushing-Main St     Arrives  16:15     2 min Tracked    07 1611+ 34H/MST
  <7>  Express  Queens             Flushing-Main St     Arrives  16:19     6 min Tracked    07 1614+ 34H/MST
 ▾  7   34 St - Hudson Yards  3 of 19
  7    Local    34 St - Hudson Yards 34 St-Hudson Yards   Arrives  16:17     4 min Tracked    07 1546+ MST/34H
  ...
 ▾  4  5  6   Uptown & The Bronx  3 of 34
  <6>  Express  Uptown & The Bronx Pelham Bay Park      Arrives  16:14     1 min Tracked    06 1557+ BBR/PEL
  4             Uptown & The Bronx Woodlawn             Arrives  16:16     3 min Tracked    04 1546+ UTI/WDL
  6    Local    Uptown & The Bronx Parkchester          Arrives  16:16     3 min Tracked    06 1602  BBR/177
 ▾  4  5  6   Downtown & Brooklyn  3 of 53
  ...
 ▸  S   Times Sq  8 trains
#+end_example

Headings name the platform when it differs from the station you chose
("42 St-Port Authority Bus Terminal · Uptown - Queens").  Directions in
which every train ends its trip, such as the S arriving at Grand
Central, are left out unless =t= shows terminating trains.  With a filter active only platforms with
matching trains are shown, so =l A= at Times Sq leaves just the A C E.

| Key           | Evil        | Command                                                           |
|---------------+-------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =TAB=         | =TAB=, =za= | Cycle the group: up to =train-time-max-rows= trains, folded, back |
| =S-TAB=       | =zM=        | Fold every group, or unfold them all                              |
| =M-n= / =M-p= | =gj= / =gk= | Next / previous group                                             |

=m= and =M-x train-time-map= use the platform at point.  Set
=train-time-board-scope= to =station= for the old one-station boards.

** Network navigator

=M-x train-time-map= draws a line as a text diagram, in the spirit of
=undo-tree-visualize=.  Stations run down the buffer; each of the
line's main stopping patterns — local, express, branches — is a
column with a node =o= where its trains stop.  =x= marks the selected
node.  Between stops, =|= runs past stations the pattern skips on the
same tracks, and =:= runs alongside another branch it does not serve.
A legend above the diagram describes each pattern and its share of
the line's scheduled trips.

#+begin_example
  A  8 Avenue Express

  A  Inwood-207 St → Ozone Park-Lefferts Blvd, 30 stops, 42% of trips
  A2 Inwood-207 St → Far Rockaway-Mott Av, 37 stops, 39% of trips
  A3 Inwood-207 St → Far Rockaway-Mott Av, 59 stops, 14% of trips

  A   A2  A3
  ...
  o---o---o  Rockaway Blvd
  |   :   :
  o   :   :  104 St
  |   :   :
  o   :   :  111 St
  |   :   :
  o   :   :  Ozone Park-Lefferts Blvd
      :   :
      o---o  Aqueduct Racetrack
      |   |
      o---o  Aqueduct-N Conduit Av
#+end_example

Below the diagram are the selected station's services, directions and
transfers: other lines stopping there or elsewhere in the same station
complex, e.g. at Times Sq-42 St (7) "N Q R W 1 2 3 S, A C E (42
St-Port Authority Bus Terminal)".

| Key                  | Evil       | Command                                    |
|----------------------+------------+--------------------------------------------|
| =C-n= / =C-p=        | =j= / =k=  | Next / previous stop on the selected service |
| =C-f= / =C-b=        | =l= / =h=  | Switch to the service right / left           |
| =M-<= / =M->=        | =gg= / =G= | First / last stop                            |
| =RET=                | =RET=      | Open the station's arrivals board            |
| =L=                  | =L=        | Show another line                            |
| =t=                  | =t=        | Transfer to another line at this station     |
| =m=                  | =m=        | Show the station on OpenStreetMap            |
| =q=                  | =q=        | Quit                                         |

=n=, =p=, =f=, =b= and the arrow keys also move, as in undo-tree.
Switching to a service that does not stop at the selected station
moves to its nearest stop.  Called from an arrivals board, the map
starts at that board's station, switching to the first line that stops
there if the line shown does not.  =L= offers the lines stopping at the
selected station first; =t= lists the transfers and moves to the
chosen line at the connected station.

=train-time-map-board-display= decides where =RET= shows the board:

- =right= (default) :: split the map window, board on the right
- =below= :: split the map window, board underneath
- =replace= :: show the board in the map's window; =q= returns to the map

With =right= and =below=, opening another station replaces the board
the map opened last instead of splitting again.

The columns come from MTA's published schedule (see [[*Line data][Line data]]), so
they show what the timetable for the current service period plans,
including long-term planned work, not the classic line map.  Rare and
short-turn patterns are left out.  The arrivals board always shows
what the real time feed says.

** Reading the display

- *Tracked* means a physical train is assigned to the trip and its
  time is a live prediction.  *Scheduled* (dimmed) means no train is
  assigned yet; the time comes from the schedule and may change.
- *Departs* is shown where the feed only gives a departure time (for
  example at a terminal); otherwise *Arrives*.
- Trains that end their trip at the station cannot be boarded and are
  hidden by default (=t= toggles them).
- *Feed time* is the timestamp MTA put on the data.  When it is older
  than =train-time-stale-threshold= the buffer says *STALE*.
- If a refresh fails, the previous predictions stay visible and the
  error is shown below them.  When a station is served by several
  feeds and only one fails, the others keep updating and the error
  names the failing feed.  The feed time shown is the oldest one.
- Times are always shown in New York time, whatever your Emacs time
  zone is.

** OpenStreetMap (osm.el)

With [[https://github.com/minad/osm][osm.el]] installed (GNU ELPA; it
needs Emacs 29+, a graphical frame and =curl=), =m= in a board or the
network navigator — or =M-x train-time-osm= — shows the line on
OpenStreetMap, centered on the station:

- The line is the one shown in the navigator, or from a board the
  first line stopping at the station.
- Its stations are osm.el pins, and each stopping pattern is a track
  segment through its stops, in a data set named after the line, e.g.
  "A 8 Avenue Express" (=M-x osm-hide= removes it).
- The track joins stations with straight segments, so it shows where
  the line runs, not its exact alignment.
- The map opens next to the train-time windows.  With both the
  navigator and a board visible it takes the bottom-right corner (below
  the board when they are side by side, right of it when they are
  stacked); with only one visible it opens to its right.  An existing
  osm.el window is reused.

Only osm.el's public commands (=osm-open= with a generated GPX file,
=osm-goto=) are used.  osm.el is optional; without it =m= just says so.

osm.el draws images, so in a text terminal =m= opens the station on
the OpenStreetMap website instead, or with =train-time-osm-terminal=
set to =geo= as a =geo:= URI that Android hands to a map app.  On
Termux this goes through =termux-open-url=; elsewhere through
=browse-url=.

** Home and work

Set your stations once, then ask for the next trains either way:

| Command                      | Does                                          |
|------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------|
| =M-x train-time-set-home=    | Choose your home station and save it          |
| =M-x train-time-set-work=    | Choose your work station and save it          |
| =M-x train-time-home-to-work= | Next trips from home to work                 |
| =M-x train-time-work-to-home= | Next trips from work to home                 |

The stations are the customizable variables =train-time-home= and
=train-time-work= (GTFS stop IDs, e.g. ="701"=), saved with Customize;
you can also set them in your init file:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq train-time-home "701"    ; Flushing-Main St
      train-time-work "R01")   ; Astoria-Ditmars Blvd
#+end_src

If one is unset, the trip commands ask for it and save it.  A trip
buffer looks like:

#+begin_example
 Leave       In Trains     Change at                  Arrive  Travel Status
 16:26   13 min  7 → W     Queensboro Plaza           17:05   38 min Scheduled
 16:30   17 min  7 → N     Queensboro Plaza           17:09   39 min Scheduled
#+end_example

| Key (Emacs / Evil) | Action                                           |
|--------------------+--------------------------------------------------|
| =g= / =g r=        | Refresh                                          |
| =RET=              | Describe each train: direction, times, stations  |
| =r=                | Reverse: work → home, or home → work             |
| =b=                | Board of the station where the trip at point starts |
| =a=                | Toggle auto refresh                              |
| =A=                | Service alerts of the trip's lines               |
| =q=                | Quit                                             |

How trips are found:

- Trips are built from the trains in MTA's real-time feeds that serve
  the home or work complex: one train that calls at both, or two trains
  with one change at a station or complex both call at.  Home and work
  mean their whole complex, so a 7 to Times Sq-42 St reaches work at
  42 St-Port Authority.
- A change needs =train-time-transfer-time= (180 s) between arriving
  and the next train leaving.  Walking between complexes, two changes
  or more, and trains not yet in the feeds (about 90 minutes ahead) are
  not considered.
- A trip is dropped when another leaves no earlier, arrives no later and
  changes no more often; up to =train-time-commute-max-rows= (12) are
  listed.  Trains whose prediction is only scheduled are dimmed.
- Times are predictions.  The list is not a promise of the fastest
  journey: delays, transfer walks and service changes are not known.

** Service alerts and elevator outages

Below a board or trip list, a footer summarizes what MTA publishes
about its lines and stations:

#+begin_example
Service alerts in effect (2):
  7 Planned - Stops Skipped: In Queens, Flushing-bound 7 skips 52 St and 69 St
  All trains at Woodside-61 St board from the Manhattan-bound platform
  7 Planned - Express to Local: In Queens, all Manhattan-bound 7 <7>
  local/express trains stop at 74 St-Broadway
5 planned service changes coming up.
  Elevator EL414 out of service (East of Main St & Roosevelt Ave to
  Manhattan-bound platform), expected back Nov 30 23:59: capital replacement
A lists the lines’ alerts.
#+end_example

- *Alerts in effect* — delays, suspensions, reroutes and planned work
  happening now, most severe first.  The mode line shows their count,
  e.g. =⚠2=.
- *Planned service changes* — planned work that has not started yet,
  counted only; =A= lists them.
- *Elevator and escalator outages* at the station or complex, current
  ones only, with the reason and when MTA expects them back.
  Outages of elevators that make the station accessible are
  highlighted.

Each alert in the footer, and the planned-changes line, is a link:
clicking (or =RET= on) one opens the alerts buffer with that alert's
details shown.  Station notices and boarding changes that name
stations are shown on those stations' boards only.  =A= (or =M-x train-time-alerts=) opens a
buffer of every alert for the lines at point; called from elsewhere or
with a prefix argument it asks for lines (empty for all):

| Key (Emacs / Evil)        | Action                              |
|---------------------------+-------------------------------------|
| =TAB=, =RET= / =za=       | Show or hide an alert's details     |
| =S-TAB= / =zM=            | Show or hide every alert's details  |
| =n= / =p= (=g j= / =g k=) | Next / previous alert               |
| =l= / =g l=               | Alerts of other lines               |
| =g= / =g r=               | Refresh                             |
| =q=                       | Quit                                |

Alerts are downloaded at most every =train-time-alerts-refresh-interval=
(120 s) and shared by all buffers; refreshing a board fetches them
again.  If they cannot be downloaded the boards still work and say so.
The list of elevators (about 650 KB, needed to know which station an
outage is at) is downloaded once a day.  Set =train-time-show-alerts=
to nil to leave all of this out.

Sources, all public and keyless: MTA's
[[https://api-endpoint.mta.info/Dataservice/mtagtfsfeeds/camsys%2Fsubway-alerts][subway alerts]]
(GTFS-Realtime with MTA's Mercury extensions) and its
[[https://api-endpoint.mta.info/Dataservice/mtagtfsfeeds/nyct%2Fnyct_ene.json][outage]]
and
[[https://api-endpoint.mta.info/Dataservice/mtagtfsfeeds/nyct%2Fnyct_ene_equipments.json][equipment]]
feeds.

** Terminal and Termux

Everything works in a text terminal (=emacs -nw=), including on an
Android phone with [[https://termux.dev][Termux]], where the screen is
narrow and a finger is the mouse.

Setup on Termux:

#+begin_src sh
pkg install emacs termux-api   # termux-api provides termux-open-url
#+end_src

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(xterm-mouse-mode 1)           ; taps become mouse clicks
(setq mouse-wheel-progressive-speed nil) ; steadier swipes
#+end_src

Termux reports a tap as a click and a swipe as the mouse wheel once
=xterm-mouse-mode= is on; the Android port of Emacs turns taps into
clicks too.  Set =COLORTERM=truecolor= for the lines' real colors.

- *Narrow windows* (under =train-time-compact-width=, 80 columns) get a
  compact layout: boards show Line, Destination, Time and In, grouped
  by direction; trip lists drop Travel and Status; text is filled to
  the window.  Buffers redraw when their window is resized.
- *Phone-width windows* (under 44 columns, e.g. a portrait phone) also
  drop the clock columns — boards show Line, Destination and In, trip
  lists In, Trains, Change at and Arrive; tap a row for exact times.
  Long headings and legends wrap, long station names end in "…", and
  messages are shortened to fit the echo area.
- *A touch bar* of buttons at the top of each buffer offers the main
  commands (Refresh, Station, Line, Direction, Alerts, Map, OSM, Quit
  and so on).  It is shown in text terminals and on Android; set
  =train-time-touch-bar= to =t= or =nil= to force it.
- *Taps:*

  | Where                 | Tap                                           |
  |-----------------------+-----------------------------------------------|
  | Board: train          | Describe the train: service, times, trip IDs  |
  | Board: destination    | Open that station's board                     |
  | Board: group heading  | More trains, then fold, then back             |
  | Navigator: station    | Select it; tap again for its board            |
  | Navigator: legend     | Follow that stopping pattern                  |
  | Navigator: title      | Show another line                             |
  | Navigator: transfer   | Show that line at this station                |
  | Trip list: trip       | Describe each train of the trip               |
  | Alerts: alert         | Show or hide its details                      |
  | Footer alert          | Open it in the alerts buffer                  |
  | "(g to retry)" etc.   | Do it                                         |

- Commands that ask for a station or line complete in the minibuffer:
  press =TAB= and tap a candidate in =*Completions*=.  Completion
  ignores case, so =times= finds "Times Sq-42 St".
- From a navigator under 80 columns wide, the board opens below it
  rather than to its right.
- =m= opens OpenStreetMap in the browser or a map app (see above).

* Design

| File                    | Role                                                     |
|-------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------|
| =train-time-pb.el=      | Schema-driven Protocol Buffers wire-format decoder        |
| =train-time-gtfs-rt.el= | GTFS-Realtime schema (+ NYCT extensions) and helpers      |
| =train-time-core.el=    | Backend-independent records (station, arrival), time fns |
| =train-time-mta-data.el= | Generated: MTA stations, routes, colors, line patterns  |
| =train-time-mta.el=     | MTA feeds per route, stations, lines, feed → arrivals     |
| =train-time.el=         | Commands, =tabulated-list-mode= UI, async HTTP, refresh   |
| =train-time-map.el=     | =M-x train-time-map= text network navigator               |
| =train-time-osm.el=     | Optional osm.el view: GPX export + =osm-open=/=osm-goto=  |
| =train-time-commute.el= | Home/work trips: one-change search over live trip updates |
| =train-time-alerts.el=  | Service alerts and elevator outages: cache, footers, list |

Principles kept so far:

- Data acquisition and normalization (=train-time-mta.el=) are separate
  from presentation (=train-time.el=); backends produce
  =train-time-arrival= records.
- Times are POSIX seconds internally and are converted to the system's
  local zone only for display.
- Missing predictions are dropped, never invented.  Skipped stops and
  zero (proto2 default) times are ignored.  The NYCT feed also carries
  "ghost" trips that passed hours ago; calls older than 30 s are
  filtered out.
- Network access goes through =train-time-fetch-function=, which tests
  replace.

A station is a GTFS parent stop such as ="701"=; its platforms add =N=
or =S=.  Stations sharing a Complex ID in Stations.csv are connected
by transfers and share one board (see [[*Station complexes][Station complexes]]).

** Line data

=train-time-mta-data.el= is generated, not written by hand, from MTA's
[[https://rrgtfsfeeds.s3.amazonaws.com/gtfs_subway.zip][static GTFS]] and
[[https://web.mta.info/developers/data/nyct/subway/Stations.csv][Stations.csv]]
by =scripts/train-time-mta-generate.el=, which is plain Emacs Lisp:

#+begin_src sh
make data      # download the current data and regenerate
#+end_src

For each line (a route plus its express variant, so 7 and 7X form one
line) it turns every scheduled trip into its sequence of stations,
weights it by the days a week it runs, drops short turns, merges
patterns that differ by one or two stops, and keeps each route's most
frequent pattern plus others with at least 5% of trips (four at most).
The file records the GTFS feed version it was built from, shown at
the bottom of the navigator.  Regenerate it when MTA publishes a new
schedule; the output is deterministic.

* Development

#+begin_src sh
make compile   # byte-compile with warnings treated as errors
make test      # ERT suite; no network access
#+end_src

Tests use recorded, unmodified MTA responses in =test/fixtures/= (the
1-7 feed, the BDFM and NQRW feeds for Coney Island, a station served by
two feeds, and the 1-7, ACE and NQRW feeds recorded together for the
Times Sq complex, the subway alerts feed and the elevator outages) and
an injected clock, so they are deterministic.  The elevator equipment
list is trimmed to the Flushing, Grand Central and Times Sq complexes.
The generator is tested against a tiny made-up GTFS feed.
